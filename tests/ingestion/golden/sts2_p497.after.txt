At the start of the Abbasid caliphate in the year 751 AD, Abu el-Ăbbas entered into Egypt with a great army to take the reign from the hands of Marwan II and his Umayyad caliphate. As the Copts were suffering intense tribulation from Marwan II, they took the side of Abu el-Ăbbas and rushed to his help. The invaders camped on the east side of the river Nile, opposite to Marwan’s forces, who were keeping the pope and many bishops under arrest on the other side. He has given orders to his men to treat the pope and his bishops with contempt, even to pluck the hair of the pope’s beard and to torture Anba Moses and humiliate them before their congregation.

The next day, he brought the pope, the bishops, and a multitude of the congregation, and left them 10 hours under the intense heat of the sun. Then, he tortured them with a vengeance; which made both the Copts and the Muslims on the Eastside, weep with bitterness; while the pope kept on praying for them.

Ăbdullah, son of Marwan II, not enduring to see that scene, wept bitterly before his father, seeking their release and telling his father that he would not be able to oppose the Khurasanians (the Abbasids) and would have to go to Sudan, where the followers of the pope most probably would not receive him well. Seeing the massive army of the Khurasanians, Marwan II was much disturbed, he had to bring the pope and his company back to Giza, where he put them chained, in four prisons, under such very intense conditions, that they were on the brink of death. However, the pope kept encouraging and comforting them.

The Khurasanians crossed over to the other bank of the Nile, defeated Marwan II, who fled, while his son went to burn the prison where the pope was kept, but once he started to do so, the enemies had him fleeing, and they put the fire out, released the prisoners, and brought them over to the St. Mark Church in Giza.

A New Tribulation:

Taking over Egypt, Abu el-Ăbbas treated the Copts well; yet only for four years, which passed like a dream. However, when he left the reign of Egypt to a new governor by the name Abu Ăwn, he treated the Copts again with viciousness, despite the pope’s attempts to remind him of how well Abu el- Ăbbas treated them, but with no avail. The Copts remained enduring bitterness until it happened that God allowed the water level of the river Nile to be two yards lower than its usual level.
