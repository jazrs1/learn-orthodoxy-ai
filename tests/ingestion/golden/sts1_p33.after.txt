ABANOUB EL-NEHISSY

(The martyr)

St. Abanoub was born in Nehissah, in the city of Talkha in the 4th Century. His parents were pious loving parents who raised him with Christian values and planted in him the love for God and the Holy Bible. He was only 12 years of age when both his parents departed to Paradise. Missing his parents was so devastating to the young Abanoub, that he was called the lamented boy for he was sorrowful for so long. Some might ask wonder how could he manage to withstand all the sufferings that he encountered later on and yet could not resist the loss of his parents.

It is true that despite our faith in heavenly life, it will not strip us from our human feelings; instead, it would sanctify them. We can see an illustration of that in the presence of St. Paul, who wrote about the door being opened for evangelism in Troas, and yet he could not find peace in his soul, for he could not find his friend Titus, so he left the city. "Furthermore, when I came to Troas to [preach] Christ's gospel, and a door was opened to me by the Lord, I had no rest in my spirit, because I did not find Titus, my brother, but taking my leave of them, I departed for Macedonia” (2 Cor. 2:12-13). This situation shows the vulnerability of human nature, and God may allow those weaknesses to prevent us from boasting. Another example of weakness was with our great father Abraham, who said that his wife Sarah was his sister for fear that the king might kill him to take her for a wife (Gen. 20:2). Also, our beloved St. Mark, who left Paul and Barnabas and caused them to have an argument and ended up separating from each other (Acts 13:3), and St. Peter, who was leaving Rome for fear of persecution and Jesus appeared to him too and kindly corrected him. Therefore, we should not be troubled when we show weakness despite the grace given to us, for it is beneficial in keeping us humble and urging us to ask God to work in us and with us.

In the village's church:

One day, Abanoub went to the church and heard the priest during the sermon encouraging the congregation to endure with joy the suffering and persecution they were getting for their faith. After the Holy Communion, the young Abanoub went home, while the priest’s words still ringing in his ears.

At home, he knelt to pray, asking for God’s guidance. Then, he stood up and started walking towards the city of Semenood (about 12 miles away), while rejoicing in the spirit and desiring to have the heavenly crown.
