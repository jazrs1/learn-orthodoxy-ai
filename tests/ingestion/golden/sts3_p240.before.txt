Their Martyrdom: 
Then, when Emperor Decius (249-251 AD) incited persecution against the 
Christians, in the days of the 14 th Pope St. Dionysius the Great (246 -264 
AD), so many believers were martyred. At that time, St. Moises sent to his 
sister to bid her fa rewell, and let her know of his decision to be martyred in 
the Name of the Lord Jesus Christ. She hastened to her mother -superior, 
sought to be released, got the blessings of her sisters, and joined her brother 
on his way to Alexandria. There, they both co nfessed together the Lord Jesus 
Christ, and after being tortured by many tools, they had their heads cut off by 
the sword, and they received the crowns of martyrdom.

MOISES, BISHOP OF OSIM, ST. 
St. Moises (Moses) was one of th e prominent fathers and saints of the 
church in the 8 th century AD. He was raised on the love of virginity and 
purity since his childhood, and he studied the church's various subjects.  
St. Moises became a monk, since his youth in the wilderness of the Sce tis, 
where he lived in the obedience of a great hermit for eighteen years, during 
which he walked along the road of virtue and harsh asceticism. He became 
known for his virtues that he was chosen to be the bishop of Osim (Ouseem), 
in the district of Embaba in the Beĥeira governorate. His life as a bishop was 
an extension of his monastic one in the wilderness. He was living in a life of 
deprivation and was known for his piety, courage, and zeal on the Orthodox 
faith. He used to spend most of his time in wors hip, that it was said that he 
did not see anyone, except on Saturdays and Sundays.
