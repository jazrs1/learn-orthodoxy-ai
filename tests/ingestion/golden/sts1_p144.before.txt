St. Amandus was the one who mentored ‘Polinus,' the future bishop of 
‘Nola,' when he was among the catechumen during the period before his 
baptism. Therefore, the two o f them were bound together by a life -long 
friendship. ‘Polinus’ wrote to him several messages that indicated how far 
‘Amandus’ has reached of piety and wisdom. In the year 400 AD, Bishop 
Delphinus reposed, and Amandus was chosen to succeed him. 
Fr. ‘Gregory’ Bishop of ‘Tours’ quoted abba ‘Polinus’ who said: “In case 
you wish to see bishops who are worthy of God, you have to look at 
‘Exceperus’ Bishop of Tolouse, ‘Simplicus’ Bishop of ‘Raphina’ and 
‘Amandius’ Bishop of ‘Bordeaux'. 
This bishop, most probably reposed on the 18th of June in the year 431 AD. 
 
 
AMATOR, THE BISHOP 
We got to know about this saint from ‘Steven’ the African priest, after the 
repose of St. Amator (Amatre) by about 160 years. Amator was the only son 
of one of the elites of the city of ‘Auxerre.' They chose for him a beautiful rich 
girl called ‘Martha’ to be his wife, despite proclaiming to his father his longing 
for a life of virginity. An old bishop by the name ‘Valerian’ came to perform 
the Sacrament of Marriage in the house. He was so advanced in age that it was 
difficult for those attending the wedding to understand his words. Holding the 
book of ministries, he read by mistake the prayers of the Deacon ’s ordination 
instead of those of the Marriage Sacrament. However, no one in the aud ience 
perceived it except the groom alone. 
When he was alone with his bride, he asked her if she has noticed what the 
bishop did! Answering in the negative, he told her what has happened. She was 
puzzled and enquired about his explanation of the situation. He responded that 
it was a sign from God of His wish for him to dedicate his life to His Savior. 
The girl threw herself into the bosom of Amator, to proclaim with joy and 
yearning that she would willingly present her life to God, along with his. 
They kept on praying to God all night long, and behold! an amazing sweet 
fragrance filled the room, described by the groom as “The fragrance of 
Paradise." Spending a long time praying and talking together in the Lord, once 
they put the light off to go to sleep, a b eautiful light shone on them, and then 
an angel came carrying two crowns to put on their heads. 
Before the end of the days of their alleged wedding, the old bishop reposed, 
and St. ‘Heladius’ was chosen to replace him. The bride and the groom set forth 
to seek his blessing and to tell him about what happened. The new bishop 
rejoiced greatly, sent ‘Martha’ to a convent, and ordained ‘Amator’ as a priest
