GABRIEL IBN NAGAĤ, THE GREAT (The martyr)

The Most Prominent of the Coptic Archons:

Gabriel ibn-Nagaĥ, also known as Yuĥanna Abu-Nagaĥ, the Great, was a prominent Copt in the 10th and the 11th centuries AD. He was the senior scribe of his time and the most prominent archon in the days of the Fatimid Caliph Al-Ĥakim bi-Amr Allah (996-1021 AD) and a contemporary of the 63rd Pope of Alexandria Abba Philotheos (979-1003 AD).

His Martyrdom:

Yuĥanna was a pious, righteous, and generous man; he was a lover of the Church and devoted to the Orthodox faith. Once the Fatimid Caliph Al-Ĥakim bi-Amr Allah, whose behavior was odd and extreme, destroyed all his prominent Muslim officers, he turned to the Coptic archons and the senior scribes. He took ten of them, Yuĥanna Abu-Nagaĥ the Great, the most senior writer, was at the top of that list, and the Caliph gave them a choice between adopting Islam or death. When the Caliph requested from him to be a Muslim to make him a minister, he asked for a day, not to think about it, but to have the chance to contact his brethren, to advise them to abide by their faith and to die in the Name of Jesus Christ. He said to them: “O my brethren, do not seek the mortal glory and lose the eternal one of the Lord Jesus Christ, who satisfied us with the goodness of the earth, and now, by His mercy, He is calling us to the kingdom of heaven. Have great courage.” His golden words, full of wisdom, brought strength to the hearts of all his listeners, who were prepared to die in the Name of Christ. He held a great banquet for them on that day, they stayed with him until sunset, and then went to their homes.

On the next day, Yuĥanna went to the Caliph, who asked him: “Nagaĥ! Have you made up your mind?” He bravely answered him: “Yes, to abide by my faith.” The ruler tried in vain with every possible way of seduction and threats to sway him away from his Christian faith and convert to Islam, but the saint was unshaken like a solid rock.

Feeling his failure, the Caliph ordered to bare the saint of his clothes, press him in the hinbazeen, then scourge him 500 whips on his back until his flesh scattered, and his blood flowed on the ground. The Caliph gave the order to keep scourging him with 500 more lashes. However, when they finished whipping him with 300 more, the saint cried out like his Lord: “I am thirsty.”
